What changed in particle transport

The variance-advection term is integrated through the equivalent normalized variable z = w/σ. The solver carries z across zero-turbulence regions instead of dividing a finite variance gradient by nearly zero variance. Crossing time uses instantaneous fluid–particle slip. Initial candidate fluctuations use a stationary Gaussian distribution.

dz = [−z/Tseen + ∇σ + σ∇lnρ] dt + √(2/Tseen) dW;   w = σz

This is the isotropic Gaussian reference closure, not a general anisotropic Reynolds-stress model. A controlled tracer check with 20,000 particles and five correlation times gives χ² = 24.23, below the 5% threshold 30.14 (19 degrees of freedom).

Orthogonal suction junctions now turn smoothly into the jet. Their section gas and powder fluxes are normalized to the solved flows; inclined junctions retain the existing reconstruction. Collision-model upgrade · numerical checks

The Exposure Lab now combines Fuchs transition Brownian encounters with WZW inertial turbulent encounters. Its legacy tracer-only option remains available for comparison. This change has 100 passing automated checks, including the retained flow, geometry and authentication tests.

  • Continuum and free-molecular Brownian limits, kernel symmetry, independent formula checks, pressure response and the removable WZW γ = 1 singularity.
  • Separate Brownian/turbulent integrals, correct first-crossing accumulation, zero-feed limits and unchanged particle paths when switching the collision kernel at the same timestep.
  • All eight STEP cases checked at 3 bar motive / 0.2 bar vessel = outlet, with finite collision components and matching home/Exposure Lab gas solutions.
  • Default timestep reduced to 0.25 µs. A representative deterministic British case agrees within 3% for integrated components when refined to 0.125 µs. This is not a convergence guarantee for every turbulent ensemble.

Physical limits remain: WZW assumes heavy point particles and locally isotropic turbulence; pair clustering is set to one. Size, drag and turbulent-Mach failures are reported as extrapolated dose. The screened subtotal is not a corrected total. No measured coagulation or final size-distribution validation is claimed, and gas-flow agreement with OpenFOAM does not validate particle collisions.

    What needs improvement first

    1. Separate suction and radial-bleed pressures. The common-pressure, complete-mixing closure misallocates air between the two sources.
    2. Resolve the internal insert and pressure recovery. Preserve the actual mixing area and expansion through successive control volumes.
    3. Locate and distribute turbulence correctly. A similar global peak can hide a displaced shear zone and incorrect section profiles.
    4. Improve particle transport and wall geometry. Add a consistent inhomogeneous stochastic drift and 3D contact geometry before interpreting wall fractions.
    5. Test withheld conditions. Obtain CFD at 3/0.2 bar and for the new STEP bodies before extending accuracy claims.

    These comparisons were not used to fit coefficients. Any future calibration should be checked against separate geometries and pressures.
