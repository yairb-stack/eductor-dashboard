How the search works and what it can establish

Use one physical shear target

A particle is exposed when its maximum sampled G = √(ε/ν) exceeds your absolute target. Useful yield counts exposed particles that reach the outlet, divided by all releases. Wall contacts and incomplete histories never count as useful delivery.

Y = Σ wᵢ I(outlet) I(max Gᵢ ≥ Gtarget)
Pᵢ = 1 − exp(−α ∫ K n dt)

Peak coverage does not impose a minimum dwell time or prove dispersion of agglomerates. The details also report integrated shear exposure. Three inlet lanes represent a specified release scenario, not a measured inlet distribution.

Keep the objectives separate

The plot retains candidates for which no other eligible setting has higher clean high-shear powder delivery per unit motive air and lower outlet coagulation. The suggested candidate maximizes this air-normalized score. Total powder stays fixed; entrained air and compressor electricity are excluded from the denominator.

Yclean ≈ Σ wᵢ I(outlet) I(max Gᵢ ≥ Gtarget) (1 − Pᵢ)
η = powder mass flow × Yclean / motive-air mass flow · g/g

Coagulation is conditional on outlet arrival; recovery is separately constrained. Growth, depletion, breakup and feedback of collisions on trajectories are not simulated here. All displayed flow and particle calculations use the Exposure Lab model.

Sampling and invalid cases

Coverage intervals use an approximate weighted Wilson calculation. Mean probability intervals use a weighted standard error. They describe sampling only, exclude model error and do not adjust for searching many settings. Numerical stops, more than 5% censored histories, fewer than four outlet samples, or failed limits exclude a candidate from recommendations. Failed gas solutions remain listed with their actual reason.

NIST: proportion intervals · Multi-objective optimization reference

Model uncertainty remains

The current CFD comparisons retain small-particle wall and residence discrepancies. The coagulation kernels can be outside their applicability range; those dose fractions are shown explicitly. A candidate with lower estimated probability still needs experimental or matched-CFD assessment. A grid search finds trade-offs within the selected ranges, not a global optimum.

Native 2 mm and 3.5 mm nozzle cases are kept separate. Earlier two-stage assemblies are excluded because they have two motive nozzles and stage-local histories. Body comparisons retain both nozzle diameter and discharge coefficient by staying within the same nozzle family. No claim of identical replaceable nozzle hardware is made.
