Less compressed air, less coagulation, less wall contact

There is no hard coagulation cutoff. Compare the non-dominated trade-offs, including mixed banks with different pressures and geometry. Air use is total motive g/s ÷ total powder g/s; lower is better.

New concept: coaxial powder-curtain cartridge

This concept feeds powder axially through a thin ring near the jet, replacing the bent side-feed architecture. It can reduce turning exposure but creates a narrower passage and greater local concentration. The calculation decides whether that trade-off helps.

New concept: annular-sheet flash cell

A wider annular sheet lowers concentration and a short flow path limits collision time. This may trade high-shear coverage for low coagulation and contact. Uniform annular feeding is assumed; the upstream powder distributor is not resolved.

New architecture: independently regulated banks

Each unit receives 1/N of the powder. Geometry and nozzle pressure may differ between banks. Air is summed across both supplies. These estimates end at separate outlets: a connecting manifold or downstream merge still needs its own model.

Display filters use the worse mean across the two closures; they are adjustable, not acceptance limits. No coagulation cutoff is imposed. This minimizes compressed-air mass, not compressor electricity. Pareto selection also retains high-shear coverage as an objective. Screening candidates need confirmation; model uncertainty is larger than the plotted sampling error.

Creative concepts that need a different flow model

Opposed free jets with a transverse powder sheet: two motive jets can put a high-shear interaction region away from walls. Their stagnation and recirculation zone could also increase residence time and coagulation. A resolved compressible impinging-jet field is needed; no performance score is invented here.

Coaxial powder core surrounded by motive jets: reversing the inlet arrangement may avoid powder-wall turns, but particles can stay sheltered in the core. It needs an annular-motive or multi-jet model, and every motive nozzle must count in air consumption.

Dilute-first, short-shear-second train: separate concentration control from exposure. It must transfer gas, pressure and particle histories between stages and count both motive supplies. This is not represented by adding independent eductor results.

Pressure control: one eductor at the array pressure

Same total powder and same nozzle pressure; total motive air is allowed to differ. Lower-pressure single units are more air-efficient in this test, while arrays reduce coagulation. This separates pressure optimization from parallelization. The active closure above applies.

Each 20°-feed unit has eight powder branches and one 3.5 mm motive nozzle; three units therefore have 24 branches and three motive nozzles. Parallel units have equal powder shares. Header distribution, splitter wall contacts and post-merge coagulation are excluded. Results therefore apply at the separate eductor exits; a real manifold can change the system ranking.

Why more units need not mean better efficiency

At unchanged pressure, N nozzles consume about N times the motive air. Powder concentration per unit falls, so coagulation can fall too. The efficiency gain must overcome the extra air.

ηN / η1 ≈ exp[Λ(1 − 1/N)] / N

Illustrative identical-path, constant-hazard limit with negligible loading changes. The calculations above solve each operating point. At fixed total air, pressure and available shear both change.

Series or a hybrid train changes the problem

A second stage sees the first stage’s gas, dilution, particle velocities and accumulated collision dose. It also adds pressure loss and wall exposure. Its inlet cannot be reset to fresh powder.

Λtotal = Λ1 + Λconnector + Λ2
ṁair,total = Σ ṁmotive,i

Series trains are not numerically ranked here. A valid comparison requires coupled pressure balance and continuous particle-history transfer, including connector contacts. Selective retreatment of a bypass stream is a candidate to test, not an established improvement.

Equations and reproducibility

Extract the source and all history ZIP files into the same folder to reproduce the full aggregation. Downloads use the same private dashboard session.

ηH = ṁpowder / Σṁmotive × mind E[1exit1max G ≥ G*]
ηclean = ṁpowder / Σṁmotive × mind E[1exit1max G ≥ G*e−Λ]

G = √(ε/ν); Λ = ∫Σⱼ Kᵢⱼnⱼ dt; sticking efficiency α = 1. Each size is evaluated against its own monodisperse background. No particle growth, background depletion, wall rebound or resuspension is modeled. The ZIP includes exact source hashes, jobs, raw terminal states, diagnostic paths and validation scripts.

Physical background: Noorani et al., particle transport in curved pipes demonstrates the importance of curvature and inertia; Balarac et al., coaxial-jet mixing demonstrates sensitivity to inlet conditions. Neither validates these proposed eductors.
