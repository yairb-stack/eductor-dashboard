What changed in particle transport

The variance-advection term is integrated through the equivalent normalized variable z = w/σ. The solver carries z across zero-turbulence regions instead of dividing a finite variance gradient by nearly zero variance. Crossing time uses instantaneous fluid–particle slip. Initial candidate fluctuations use a stationary Gaussian distribution.

dz = [−z/Tseen + ∇σ + σ∇lnρ] dt + √(2/Tseen) dW;   w = σz

This is the isotropic Gaussian reference closure, not a general anisotropic Reynolds-stress model. A controlled tracer check with 20,000 particles and five correlation times gives χ² = 24.23, below the 5% threshold 30.14 (19 degrees of freedom).

Orthogonal suction junctions now turn smoothly into the jet. Their section gas and powder fluxes are normalized to the solved flows; inclined junctions retain the existing reconstruction. The British reference feeder uses its measured CAD taper, including the inward velocity required by continuity. The native-CAD comparison below is separate from the browser’s reduced-wall model.

What remains before a matched particle validation

The recovered reference is stochastic Lagrangian tracking on an SST mean field, not resolved turbulent DNS. Its protocol differs: stationary initial fluctuations, fixed drag properties, scalar crossing time, no gravity, native wall envelope and a 50 ms horizon. The dashboard uses zero initial fluctuations, local properties, anisotropic crossing times, gravity and reduced walls. A four-way field/tracker comparison must hold wall geometry fixed first; a separate wall swap then tests geometry. That experiment is not presented as completed.

Heavy-particle drift damping, a diffuser separation closure, field interpolation tables and near-wall corrections are unvalidated proposals. They have not been fitted to reproduce contact percentages. Exact caching and the existing CPU worker pool remain active.

New coagulation option: Experiment → Particles → Brownian + Saffman–Turner. It adds Fuchs Brownian encounters to the small-inertia turbulent shear kernel. The selected kernel also drives optional population growth. Inertial and clustering extensions are disabled for this reference option; outside-assumption dose remains visible.

Collision-model upgrade · numerical checks

The Exposure Lab now combines Fuchs transition Brownian encounters with WZW inertial turbulent encounters. Its legacy tracer-only option remains available for comparison. This change has 100 passing automated checks, including the retained flow, geometry and authentication tests.

  • Continuum and free-molecular Brownian limits, kernel symmetry, independent formula checks, pressure response and the removable WZW γ = 1 singularity.
  • Separate Brownian/turbulent integrals, correct first-crossing accumulation, zero-feed limits and unchanged particle paths when switching the collision kernel at the same timestep.
  • All eight STEP cases checked at 3 bar motive / 0.2 bar vessel = outlet, with finite collision components and matching home/Exposure Lab gas solutions.
  • Default timestep reduced to 0.25 µs. A representative deterministic British case agrees within 3% for integrated components when refined to 0.125 µs. This is not a convergence guarantee for every turbulent ensemble.

Physical limits remain: WZW assumes heavy point particles and locally isotropic turbulence; pair clustering is set to one. Size, drag and turbulent-Mach failures are reported as extrapolated dose. The screened subtotal is not a corrected total. No measured coagulation or final size-distribution validation is claimed, and gas-flow agreement with OpenFOAM does not validate particle collisions.

What changed in upgrades 1–5

  1. CAD suction circuit

    The eight STEP assemblies supply actual inner passage diameters and swept centrelines. A common feeder and separate branch resistances determine the flow split. Each radial hole has its own inlet restriction. The four-suction paths have unequal lengths. Symmetric bleed holes still share one circumferential mean pressure; swirl and unequal local hole pressure are unresolved.

  2. Pressure and entrainment

    Mass, axial momentum and total energy are closed against the shared vessel/outlet pressure. A finite mixing length supplies separate suction and bleed pressures; a sonic mixing / normal-shock diffuser branch is tried when needed. This remains a steady reduced balance, not a solution of recirculation, shock cells or signed reverse-flow transport. The historical British body uses a documented calibration; the CAD cases do not inherit that fit.

  3. Spatial shear

    Spreading responds to turbulence intensity, integral length and convective Mach. The peak, peak position and radial shear width were fitted on the accepted British 3.5 mm gas case; the 2.5 mm case was held out. Mean velocity gradient and G = √(ε/ν) are separate outputs. Global turbulent dissipation is limited by available mechanical power. The holdout peak location and downstream shear profiles still need improvement.

  4. 3D particle transport

    Particles follow all passage paths in x, y and z, with three turbulent velocity components and powder-flux-weighted statistics. Internal junctions use approximate tube unions. Tracking ends at first wall contact; deposition and rebound are explicitly unknown. Full CAD surface collision, bleed-hole escape and whole-train particle transfer remain outside this version.

  5. Local dilution

    The radial/azimuthal concentration reconstruction is normalized by gas flux, so integrating C u over a section returns the admitted powder flow. Local concentration drives collision exposure; the dilution chart shows section averages. After the last inlet the powder/air mass ratio is conserved. Local mixing is a closure assumption, not independently validated concentration data.

Next validation targets: spatial shear and residence distributions, real internal wall collision, well-mixed turbulent dispersion, finite-inertia collision kernels, and measured adhesion/breakup. The printed ¾″ source has an invalid solid and needs CAD repair before reliable CFD meshing.

Framework references: 1D ejector networks and turbulent scalar/velocity structure. These motivate reduced closures; they do not validate this implementation.

Improve the mixing model before fitting one correction factor

The archived comparisons show systematic errors in suction, bleed and mixing geometry. Failed CFD runs identify gaps in the reference calculation; they cannot establish the analytical error at those conditions. This section records the pre-upgrade review. The revision-2 validation table above reports the implemented changes and their current limits. Inspect the CFD cases and source records ↗

What the usable comparisons show

  • 45° pressure sweep: predicted feeder flow is 27.4%, 32.7% and 36.2% low at 2, 3 and 5 bar motive pressure; reservoir = 1 bar absolute.
  • Mixing diameter matters: feeder error changes from +82.2% at Ø10 mm to −45.8% at Ø40 mm. A common multiplier would worsen one end.
  • British 3.5 mm: motive error −3.6%, feeder −64.3%, bleed +107.3%, outlet +21.3%. The wrong air split matters more than nozzle mass flow alone.
  • Shear location: the British 3.5 mm analytical peak occurs at x/D = 4.06 versus 2.04 in the archived CFD; peak magnitude is 8.9% low. Matching one peak value will not fix particle exposure.

Errors = 100 × (analytical − CFD) / CFD. Gas-only conditions; historical CAD is simplified in the analytical closure. These comparisons do not isolate a unique physical cause and are not experimental validation.

Why references were excluded

  • C–D CAD mesh: latest British original and 1″ meshes have outlet-area errors of +94.7% and +96.7%. Repair and verify the connected fluid domain before comparing flow.
  • Vacuum startup: several 3 / 0.2 bar runs terminate with floating-point exceptions. Ramped startup improves some runs, but no completed averaging window exists.
  • Incomplete timing: the longest new run reaches 1.764 ms; the required comparison mean is 6–10 ms. Continue from a verified checkpoint and assess stationarity before acceptance.
  • Archived exclusions: air-film has field bounding during averaging; early-dilution misses mass balance and stationarity; the old Ø40 mm run has 14.4% feeder drift. Two other entries are superseded revisions, not failed physics.

OpenFOAM’s “exception trapping enabled” startup message is not itself a failure. Archive diagnostics are checked separately from run completion and acceptance.

What the analytical model solves

1 · Airflow and spatial field

By default, revision 2 solves the suction network and reduced pressure / entrainment balances using the selected geometry, motive supply, vessel pressure, outlet pressure and powder feed. Feeder air, British bleed air and chamber pressure are outputs. The two-stage solution transfers gas mass, temperature and interstage pressure between stages. Unsupported solutions remain unavailable. An explicitly selected prescribed-flow mode is available for sensitivity scenarios, with no automatic fallback. Nozzle mass flow uses the solved suction-section pressure, or the explicitly prescribed chamber pressure.

A spreading Gaussian axial profile is normalized to the specified section mass flow. Its excess velocity decays as 1/b, with b = D/√2 + c x, and is bounded to avoid negative annular velocity. Radial velocity follows axisymmetric mass continuity away from the side junction. Extracted curved passage centrelines supply the 3D branch field. Junctions and the radial/azimuthal scalar distribution are closure assumptions; the model does not resolve recirculation or shock-particle dynamics.

2 · Turbulent shear

The fluctuation scale peaks in the jet shear layer. Its development and spread are linked to the turbulence controls; historical British coefficients are calibrated to one gas case and checked on a different nozzle size. k = 3σ²/2 and ε = 0.9 k3/2/ℓ, with ℓ set as a fraction of jet width. The integrated axisymmetric main-jet dissipation is capped by motive mechanical power minus estimated outlet kinetic power. This is an energy ceiling, not a complete coupled energy solution.

Gref is the sampled maximum G at x/D > 1 in this analytical field, unless the fixed slide reference is selected. Spatial sampling is finite. The coefficient I and integral length are uncertain and exposed as controls.

3 · Particle motion

dx/dt = v (three components)
dv/dt = (U + u′ − v) / τp + g
τp = ρpdp² Cc / (18 μ fRe)
fRe = 1 + 0.15 Re0.687 (Re < 1000)
Cc = 1 + Kn[1.257 + 0.4 exp(−1.1/Kn)]
Kn = 2λ / dp

Local p and T set density, Sutherland viscosity, mean free path and slip correction. Optional fluctuations follow a local Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with TL = 0.4 σ²/ε. The drag step uses exponential relaxation and adaptive spatial/turbulent limits. This simple noise model lacks a full well-mixed drift and wall anisotropy; the candidate uses well-mixed drift and crossing-trajectory decorrelation. Relative-Mach drag remains outside this revision. Brownian spatial diffusion is omitted.

4 · Exposure statistics

G = √(ε / ν)
tq = ∫ 1[G(t) ≥ q Gref] dt
ηq = √[νref / (q Gref)]
Nℓ(q) = q Gref tq min[1,(ηq/dp)2/3]

Threshold crossings are interpolated within a step. νref is evaluated at this model’s jet shear maximum; the archived slide instead used a high-shear-cell median from CFD. The count uses the slide’s unit turnover prefactor. Misses and early wall contacts remain in group statistics. SD describes the chosen stochastic model, not uncertainty in the physics.

Scope. The solved gas balance includes a bulk powder momentum term. Individual particle histories remain one-way coupled: they do not update the spatial gas field. Prescribed-flow mode does not solve the pressure balance. Multi-suction cases use all extracted branch paths and hydraulic flow splitting. Two-stage selections start a new particle release in the chosen stage. Bleed-hole escape, rebound, adhesion, breakup and full-train particle transfer are not included.
